# ISS-006 08Z-cohort day-N+2+ silent-at-nominal regime has a second sub-shape — four-day alternating silent/manual-dispatch interleave with decelerating manual drift, distinct from the 10-10→10-12 continuous-manual-with-accelerating-drift shape

**Why:** The 10-29 → 11-01 four-day sequence (10-29 fully silent, 10-30 manual +60m, 10-31 fully silent, 11-01 manual +40m) extends the day-N+2+ silent-at-nominal regime from [[iss-006-08z-nominal-silence-persists-past-day-n-plus-2]] (n=3 at 10-10/11/12) to n=4 but with a novel structure: operator manual-dispatch cadence is **every other day** rather than every day, so two fully-silent intermediate days (10-29, 10-31) sit between the two manual-surface days (10-30, 11-01), and the manual drift **decelerated** +60m → +40m rather than accelerating +55m → +93m as in 10-11 → 10-12. The first-at-dispatch >36h heartbeat self-check breach also now persists across *two* fully-silent intermediate days (10-30 at 48h 6m, 11-01 at 47h 34m with 10-31 fully silent), so the >36h threshold loses fidelity entirely under this variant: a continuous 4+ day silent-at-nominal window reports only ~48h regardless of total duration.

**How to apply:** when the day-N+2 silent-at-nominal regime starts, do not assume manual-dispatch cadence will be daily — the operator may intervene only every other day, which doubles the gap between surface points without changing the underlying pocket state. For `cron-state.json` delta accounting, preserve EVERY day's state (manual OR fully-silent OR both-absent) rather than the most-recent-fire-wins reading per [[iss-006-08z-nominal-silence-persists-past-day-n-plus-2]]'s "How to apply" clause; otherwise the 48h breach ceiling masks the true outage duration. For the per-slot cron rewrite, model operator-manual-dispatch cadence as an exogenous input to the Markov chain, not a function of pocket state — the operator's intervention schedule can bifurcate the day-N+2+ regime into continuous-daily and alternating-every-other-day sub-shapes with different drift directions (accelerating vs. decelerating).
